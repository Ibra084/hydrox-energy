How it works

Stage 1 today: firm power in, hydrogen out.

Barakah runs at about 90% capacity factor, day and night. Hydrox decides how much of that firm output becomes hydrogen.

Step 1 · 1,337 MWe

Firm power is allocated

Nuclear electricity plus low-grade steam (<200°C) from the secondary loop. Hydrox proposes an allocation; the reactor is untouched and operates normally with Hydrox disconnected.

Step 2 · ~800 °C

Steam is split

Solid-oxide electrolysis. The stack's operating temperature is reached electrically with heat recuperation, not drawn from reactor coolant. Running hot is what cuts the electricity bill per kilogram.

Step 3 · H₂

Hydrogen is delivered

Product boundary: hydrogen at ~20–30 bar at the plant gate. Compression, storage and conversion to ammonia are modelled as optional downstream modules.

Next · Stages 2 and 3

Same Hydrox layer throughout.

Stage 2: an HTGR supplies genuine 750°C process heat, so heat replaces more of the electricity. Stage 3: above 850°C, thermochemical cycles remove most of the electrical demand entirely.

Proven foundations

Every stage is proven, at its own scale.

Nuclear hydrogen is already commercial

001
Stage 1 · Nine Mile Point · USA · 2023
Evidence

Constellation has produced hydrogen from nuclear electricity since March 2023: a 1.25 MW electrolyser making 560 kg/day at an existing reactor.

Scale

Commercial, with no plant modification.

The pathway is modelled and tested

002
Stage 1 · Idaho National Laboratory
Evidence

DOE's lead nuclear lab develops high-temperature steam electrolysis and supplied the dynamic controller for the Nine Mile Point demonstration.

HTSE model

36.8 kWh electricity + 6.4 kWh heat per kg, against ~50 kWh/kg for PEM.

SHEET 07 / UAE
National benefits

Why this matters for the UAE.

01 · Stage 1

No new reactors required

Hydrox attaches to reactors the UAE already operates. Nuclear-powered electrolysis is proven commercially at existing plants.

02

A named customer

ADNOC's Ta'ziz ammonia facility at Ruwais: 1 Mtpa capacity, roughly 177,000 t/yr of hydrogen demand. A 65,000 t/yr facility covers about a third of one domestic customer.

03

Aligned with national targets

65,000 t/yr ≈ 4.6% of the UAE's 2031 low-carbon hydrogen production target (1.4 Mtpa), drawing ~23% of one Barakah unit's output.

Example · UAE

ENEC has stated Barakah's potential at around 1 Mtpa of hydrogen, and its ADVANCE programme is already evaluating high-temperature reactors for steam, heat and hydrogen.

SHEET 10 / FOUNDERS
Founders' note

Why we're building Hydrox.

Barakah already produces firm, low-carbon power around the clock. We kept asking why that hasn't turned into cheap clean hydrogen, and found the gap is less about reactors than about how their energy is allocated. Hydrox is our attempt to close it: a control layer that is honest about its assumptions, never touches the reactor, and is proven in simulation before anyone builds anything.

We're at the concept and simulation stage, and there is a lot we don't know yet. If you work in nuclear, process engineering or controls and can help us find out, we'd like to hear from you.
